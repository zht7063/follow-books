Begin with the person

Dan Koe's homepage presents a writer concerned with how people think, work and create a life. Its identity section connects The Art of Focus with Eden and describes writing as part of a creative livelihood. That public self-description, reviewed in this run, is a better starting point than treating a handful of AI articles as his whole subject.

This edition follows three intersecting questions: what is worth learning, how can one person make useful work, and where do tools help? These are our organizing questions. They are not a table of contents supplied or endorsed by Dan.

What kind of book is this?

A collection can be useful without pretending to be complete. These chapters are original editorial: short arguments, reading questions and routes to sources. They do not reproduce Dan's essays. Read an original, return here to compare it with another, and keep your own notes on where the argument holds up.

The seed was compiled around technology and products. Its older Chinese abstracts and inherited reading claims are omitted from this reader and search. We preserve that selection's limits instead of silently converting it into a comprehensive portrait. Early GitHub references have an uncertain identity connection; their presence in an old index does not establish a current engineering practice.

Use the labels as part of reading

A fetch label describes collection. A read label describes review. A public excerpt review means only the specified portion was examined. Seed titles, tags and dates are prior metadata, and a product list can tell us that a title exists without telling us the article's argument. Unknown dates remain unknown, while dated entries retain the seed's explanation of what the date measures.

Treat commercial self-descriptions as attributed claims. This reader has not purchased a course, tested a paid product or independently verified business revenue. The homepage establishes how Dan describes his work; it does not validate every promise made by that work.
